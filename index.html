Our Story

Three Generations of the Trade

Wattie Crawford opened the shop in 1948, fresh out of the army with a set of knives and a lease on a wee unit halfway up Duke Street. He sold to his neighbours, took his cuts seriously, and never once put anything on the counter he wouldn't feed his own family.

Seventy-odd years on, his grandson Davie runs the block. The tiles are the same, the sawdust's long gone, but the standard hasn't shifted an inch. We still hang our beef on the bone, still buy whole carcasses from farms we've dealt with for decades in Ayrshire and Perthshire, and still link the morning's sausages by hand.

— Davie Crawford Third-generation master butcher

The House Speciality

Aged on the Bone, 35 Days

In the cold room at the back, prime Scotch hindquarters hang on the old steel rail — losing water, gaining flavour, the fat turning to butter. It's slower, it costs us more, and it's the reason folk cross the city for a Crawford steak.
